## Platform

web | app | both. One line.

## Stack

Languages, delivery and constraints (for example: static HTML/CSS/JS, CDN only, no build step; how the
deck is exported; where it is hosted).

## Users

- **Primary:** who decides or acts, and the scene: room, device, screen size, time available, language.
- **Secondary:** who forwards, champions or maintains it, and how material reaches them (email, chat).
- **Tertiary:** anyone who touches only part of it (operators, cashiers, support).

## Product Purpose

What the product does in two or three sentences, why it exists, and what success looks like for the
presentation and for the product.

## Positioning

What it is relative to existing tools or approaches, and the one claim that competitors cannot copy.

## Operating Context

How material reaches the audience (meeting cadence, forwarded images, projection in a bright room),
the source documents, and who the offering party is.

## Capabilities and Constraints

What the product can do, what it cannot, language rules for outward material, rules for figures (source
and quoting), data labelling (for example "sample data" on every simulated screen), and what is still
undecided.

## Brand Commitments

Whose identity leads. Client colours and logos: where they may appear (logos and chrome only, never
the page palette). Prior work that is reusable, and prior work whose look is not inherited.

## Evidence on Hand

What exists (documents, photos, data) and what is absent and must not be fabricated (logos, real data,
testimonials, prices).

## Product Principles

3 to 6 numbered principles. Each one is a rule a screen can be checked against, for example "every
surface answers X before anything else". Not slogans.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Minimum contrast, how colour-blind users are served (shape or label), motion rules, and the viewing
distance the type sizes assume.
